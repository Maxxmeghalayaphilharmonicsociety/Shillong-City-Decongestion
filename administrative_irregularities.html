OFFICIAL INVESTIGATIVE COMPILATION

Administrative Irregularities, Statutory Bypass, and Environmental Violations in Meghalaya Tourism Projects

A comprehensive cross-verification of procedural oversteps, judicial contradictions, tribal governance workarounds, and national safety violations across the ₹330-crore Taj Umiam Resort & Spa redevelopment and the ₹151-crore Shillong Peak Ropeway Project.

📊 Executive Summary

This is a compilation of documented procedural oversteps, judicial contradictions, tribal governance workarounds, and national safety violations committed by the Meghalaya state administration.

It exposes a systemic pattern of overriding local community consent, manipulating regulatory frameworks, and bypassing ecological safeguards across two multi-million-dollar flagship tourism projects: the ₹330-crore Taj Umiam Resort & Spa redevelopment and the ₹151-crore Shillong Peak Ropeway Project.

I Part I: The Historical Pollution of the Original State-Run Orchid Resort

Prior to its commercial transfer, the state government operated under a severe conflict of interest, serving simultaneously as the developer, the polluter, and the environmental regulator.

1. The Statutory Closure Notice

Long before the private handover, the Meghalaya State Pollution Control Board (MSPCB) was legally forced to issue severe statutory closure notices to its own state-owned flagship properties, including the Orchid Lake Resort, for operating without functional effluent treatment infrastructure [Cobrapost].

2. Judicial Enforcement (2022)

On October 20, 2022, the High Court of Meghalaya formally halted construction and intervened against prominent lakeside entities in the PIL In Re: Cleanliness of Umiam Lake, specifically naming the state-run Orchid Lake Resort[cite: 1]. The bench documented that the resort lacked adequate mechanisms to handle commercial effluents and garbage, allowing raw waste to seep directly into the primary water supply[cite: 1].

II Part II: The National Dam Safety Act Violations & Structural Risks (Umiam)

The central pillar of the engineering and energy security challenge rests on the fact that Umiam Lake is a critical, active hydroelectric reservoir housing the Umiam Stage I powerhouse, which has been operational since 1965. Activists and engineering watchdogs have exposed major violations under the central Dam Safety Act, 2021 framework:

1. Encroachment on High-Risk Inundation Zones

The Act mandates strict surveillance and vulnerability mapping of major dams. Heavy commercial construction is heavily restricted within high-risk margins to avoid catastrophic failures. Ignoring these boundaries on active reservoir banks directly violates safety mandates.

2. The "Grandfathered" Renovation Loophole

To avoid a fresh federal Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) required for a new mega-hospitality footprint, the administration legally framed the layout as an "upgradation" or "renovation" of the old Orchid property, shielding the luxury expansion from scrutiny.

3. Siltation Acceleration

Under Dam Safety Act and DRIP guidelines, the state must prioritize actions reducing reservoir siltation. Heavy earth-cutting and clearing on fragile reservoir banks directly accelerate soil erosion into the waterbed, violating statutory obligations.

4. The Structural Integrity Contradiction

The Umiam Dam requires emergency rehabilitation (grouting, 12 MT weight limits) under DRIP. Opponents highlight the contradiction of spending public funds on emergency grouting while clearing a private mega-project introducing heavy commercial traffic along the rim.

III Part III: Contradictions in the Eco-Clearances & Buffer Zones

Official Right to Information (RTI) records exposed a major gap between the government's public statements and its internal, legally binding approvals.

1. The Provisional Document Scam

To quiet public anger, the Tourism Department admitted that the Consent to Establish (CTE) issued by the MSPCB was merely a provisional document. This confirmed that the state executive handed over the land and held a grand foundation ceremony before securing final, unconditional environmental clearances[cite: 3].

2. The Lumpongdeng Island Deception

The Green-Tech Foundation exposed that while the Chief Minister promised the public that Lumpongdeng Island would see no permanent structural damage, the official CTE issued to Umiam Hotels Pvt. Ltd. clearly authorized the permanent construction of luxury villas, restaurants, and cafes directly on the eco-fragile island[cite: 3].

3. Manipulation of Judicial Buffer Zones

In May 2023, the High Court placed a strict ban on any new constructions within 50 metres of the high-water mark[cite: 2]. To shield its public-private partnership (PPP) venture, the administration structured the Meghalaya Waterbodies (Preservation and Conservation) Guidelines, 2023 to establish relaxed localized zoning boundaries, ensuring the luxury layout would technically bypass standard encroachment violations.

IV Part IV: Sixth Schedule & KHADC Land Law Violations

The administrative transfers across both tourism projects violated the tribal sovereignty protections guaranteed by the Sixth Schedule of the Indian Constitution.

1. Circumvention of Tribal Consent (Umiam)

The Meghalaya Tourism Development Corporation (MTDC) executed the 60-year commercial lease with external, private corporate bodies without obtaining a binding No-Objection Certificate (NOC) from the Khasi Hills Autonomous District Council (KHADC) and the local Dorbar Shnong (traditional village councils).

2. The Institutional Warfare at Shillong Peak

This exact same bypass resulted in direct physical and legal clashes at the Shillong Peak Ropeway lower terminal site. On October 27, 2026, the KHADC Enforcement Wing aggressively locked and sealed the ropeway's terminal site. The council evicted project staff, citing that the state's chosen executing firms (KEC International and Tantia Construction) were operating illegally without a mandatory Trading License for Non-Tribals under the United Khasi-Jaintia Hills District Regulation, 1954.

3. The State Retaliation

Rather than resolving the constitutional compliance issue, the state's Tourism Department openly defied the council, filing a police FIR against the KHADC Enforcement Inspector for trespass and property seizure, claiming state-owned property is exempt from tribal regulation. This mirrors the exact retroactive compliance arguments used to preserve the Umiam lease agreements.

V Part V: Corroborative Manipulations in the Shillong Ropeway Project

The administrative manipulation strategy re-emerges entirely within the ₹151-crore Shillong Peak Ropeway Project, exposing a state-wide blueprint for overriding ecological and civic safeguards:

1. Abuse of the Meghalaya Ropeway Act, 2022

Under Section 36 of the Act, the state granted the executive ultimate powers to permit private promoters to string cables over private land, engineered specifically to suppress property objections and bypass traditional rights.

2. Fabrication of Community Consent

To meet baseline guidelines, the state cited a 2022 public consultation. MIG minutes revealed only 27 people attended (5 being state staff), and while 80% warned of groundwater devastation, the state buried objections to claim unanimous consent.

3. Violation of Critical Catchments

Just as Umiam was endangered, the ropeway cuts directly through the U Lum Shyllong area, a protected catchment basin housing primary natural aquifers supplying drinking water to South Shillong.

4. The Deforestation Proof

Despite claims of a zero-impact eco-friendly venture, government records admitted that at least 545 trees have been felled directly within the fragile water catchment corridor to clear the 20-metre right-of-way.

VI Part VI: Current Status & Executive Retractions

Following sustained civil blockades, joint action committee protests, and environmental resistance, the state executive has been forced into partial legal rollbacks:

1. Mainland Umiam Containment Order

The state officially decreed that all heavy five-star resort infrastructure will be strictly restricted to the pre-existing mainland footprint of the old Orchid Resort[cite: 4]. Lumpongdeng Island (36.10 acres) was scaled back to low-impact, temporary footprints under civil pressure[cite: 4].

2. The Ropeway Deadlock

The Shillong Peak lower terminal remains a flashpoint of criminal cases and turf disputes between the state police and the KHADC tribal enforcement team over non-tribal trading licenses.

3. The Transparency Vacuum

The administration remains under intense pressure to release the comprehensive White Paper promised by Chief Minister Conrad Sangma to address financial and statutory anomalies across these public asset handovers[cite: 5].
