Forensic Audit & Fact-Check Report: Shillong Peak Ropeway Project

Independent cross-verification of internal project files, statutory notifications, and official November 2026 public consultation statements against publicly accessible records from the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEF&CC), the New Development Bank (NDB), the Ministry of DoNER, and the Government of Meghalaya.

Executive Audit Conclusion

A forensic audit comparing internal project documents against official public-domain filings confirms major institutional contradictions. Public records verify that the 955-meter ropeway underwent federal Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) appraisal under Schedule Activity 7(g) in 2021, was formally pitched to both domestic urban planners and multilateral lenders as a road-traffic and greenhouse-gas emissions reduction measure, and faces direct statutory, co-financing, hydrological, and building-code conflicts.

01

Environmental Clearance (EIA) & Regulatory Appraisal

VERIFIED FALSE CLAIM

Official Claim (Nov 2026)

During the November 2026 public consultations, state representatives asserted that an Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) was never applicable or required for the ropeway project.

Internal Document Finding

Page 32 of the November 2022 Meghalayan Age Limited (MAL) / MIG Public Consultation Report explicitly lists "EIA Clearances" as the very first mandatory statutory approval required before construction can begin.

Public-Domain Fact-Check

Official MoEF&CC records on the PARIVESH portal confirm the 955-meter Proposed Shillong Peak Ropeway (Riat Laban/Lawsohtun to Shillong Peak) was appraised by the Expert Appraisal Committee (Infra-2) at its 66th Meeting and recommended for EC at its 73rd Meeting (September 2021) under Schedule Item 7(g): Aerial Ropeways of the EIA Notification, 2006.

Critical Legal & Technical Nuance: While initial Environmental Clearance (EC) and Stage-I/II Forest Clearance (for 2.3 ha) were processed for the baseline 2021 scope (then estimated at ₹83.56 crore), major changes in project cost (escalating to ₹151 crore), massive multi-story commercial construction at both terminals (G+4 at LTP and G+3 at UTP), conflicting vertical rise figures (343m in Table 8 vs. 318m on p. 25), and unresolved community spring/aquifer objections trigger mandatory scrutiny under EC compliance and amendment norms.
02

The "Decongestion" & Urban Mobility Contradiction

VERIFIED FALSE CLAIM

Official Claim vs. Internal DPR: Officials stated at the November 2026 consultation that traffic decongestion was never part of the project rationale or policy documentation. Yet Page 30 of the 2022 MIG Report explicitly states the project will "reduce the number of vehicle movement from Shillong town to Shillong Peak which is about 15 KM from the town reducing significantly the source of emission."

Shillong Urban Mobility Policy (2024)

Published by the Meghalaya Urban Affairs Department (Section 6.2, Public Transport), the policy explicitly integrates "ropeways, cable cars, vertical elevators" as long-term alternative public transit interventions to relieve congestion across Shillong's steep terrain (70-minute daily traffic snarls, sub-15 km/h speeds, and ₹500 crore annual congestion loss).

NDB Sovereign Loan Filing (July 18, 2022)

Under the Project Objective section of the New Development Bank's public disclosure for the Meghalaya Ecotourism Infrastructure Development Project, the government officially committed that: "The Project will also contribute to reduced greenhouse gas emissions through replacement of road travels by the ropeway."

Infrastructure Reality on the Ground: Despite this public transit framing to central and multilateral funders, the alignment connects a residential foothill strictly to a restricted tourist viewpoint—and Table 12 of the 2022 MIG Report allocates 258 to 305 four-wheeler parking spaces at the Lower Terminal Point (LTP) in Lawsohtun/Riat Laban, funneling and concentrating tourist vehicular traffic inside narrow residential neighborhoods rather than removing cars from Shillong's road network.

03

Co-Financing & Non-Duplication Rules (NDB vs. PM-DevINE)

VERIFIED REGULATORY CONFLICT

Public reporting and official briefings confirm the ₹151 crore project is co-financed through ₹80.89 crore from the New Development Bank (NDB) and ₹70.11 crore from the Ministry of DoNER's PM-DevINE scheme. Cross-checking the NDB loan disclosure against the PM-DevINE scheme guidelines exposes a direct compliance conflict:

1. New Development Bank (NDB) Disclosure

Approved on July 18, 2022 (Total NDB Sovereign Loan: USD 79.05 million), the NDB project summary explicitly lists the "Shillong Peak ropeway" under Component (iii) (connecting infrastructure to improve access to tourist attraction points).

2. PM-DevINE Non-Duplication Mandate

Under Section 2.2 and Section 7.1.5(ii) (Negative List) of the PM-DevINE Guidelines, central grants are strictly barred for "Projects for which the concerned line Ministry has already provided funds or plans to provide funds (non-duplication)." Annexure-C (Item 7c) mandates a signed Non-Duplication Certificate prior to sanction.

Splitting a single 955-meter ropeway asset across an externally aided NDB sovereign loan and a central PM-DevINE grant creates direct audit exposure unless an explicit scope bifurcation and exemption were formally recorded by the Empowered Inter-Ministerial Committee (EIMC) under Para 12 of the guidelines.

04

Statutory Compliance Under the Meghalaya Ropeway Act, 2022

ACT NO. 14 OF 2022

The Meghalaya Ropeway Act, 2022 (Act No. 14 of 2022), notified by the Law Department on September 27, 2022 (effective September 23, 2022), imposes binding pre-construction safeguards across 65 sections:

Statutory Provision Legal Requirement in Public Act Audit Finding / Compliance Gap
Section 3(2) & Section 2(k) Requires the Authority to formulate a Vision Paper at the developmental planning stage in consultation with the "Local Authority"—explicitly defined in Section 2(k) to include traditional heads: Syiem, Lyngdoh, and Rangbah Shnong. Consultation with traditional Dorbars occurred reactively during construction disputes in 2026 rather than through a pre-planning statutory Vision Paper.
Sections 26, 29 & 30 Prohibits construction without a formal Licence (Sec. 30(2)). Requires publishing the draft licence in local and national newspapers, allowing 30 days for objections, serving direct notice on every landowner/occupier along the route (Sec. 29(3)), and uploading the final licence online (Sec. 30(3)). Publicly verifiable copies of the statutory 30-day draft licence newspaper notices, individual Right-of-Way (ROW) landowner notices, and online Construction Licence remain unproduced.
Sections 34 & 35 Mandates gazetted constitution of a District Project Committee (chaired by the Deputy Commissioner with local community members) to oversee implementation and resolve grievances. Traditional bodies and the Joint Action Committee (JAC) have been excluded from formal statutory committee governance.
Section 36(2)–(4) Executing works or suspending cables over immovable property despite objections requires a formal written order and prior payment of compensation/annual rent determined by the Deputy Commissioner as a condition precedent. The 19,100 sq. m. aerial corridor traverses community and protected forest zones without documented Section 36 compensation orders.
05

Building Bye-Laws, Cost Anomalies & Geospatial Vetting

MULTIPLE BREACHES

A. Building Bye-Law Violations (Verified via Gazette of Meghalaya, Sept 11, 2025)

Under the Meghalaya Building (Second Amendment) Bye-Laws, 2025 (published in the Gazette of Meghalaya, Part-IIA, September 11, 2025), expedited self-certification and third-party plan approvals apply strictly to Low-Risk buildings (up to 2,500 sq. ft. plot / 3,000 sq. ft. built-up area, max 7m height) and Medium-Risk buildings (up to G+2 floors / 750 sq. m. built-up area), and Clause J.2(f) explicitly excludes State Government projects.

Yet the Lower Terminal Point (LTP) proposes a G+4 commercial-cum-terminal complex (20m height, 10,000 sq. m. total covered area) housing a 3rd-floor commercial food court and recreational complex, while the Upper Terminal Point (UTP) proposes a G+3 structure (4,800 sq. m. covered area) inside a protected forest zone. Both are High-Risk public infrastructure buildings requiring full statutory municipal/MUDA scrutiny, and planners excluded 6,000 sq. m. of multi-level parking across three floors to claim an artificial Floor Space Index (FSI) of 2.0 on a 4,000 sq. m. plot.

B. Cost Escalation (+80.7%) & Broken Arithmetic (2022 MIG Cost Sheet, pp. 33–34)

  • Escalation Timeline: Appraised at ₹83.56 crore in the 2021 MoEF&CC Form-1 filing, escalated to ₹108.06 crore in the November 2022 MIG Report, and inflated to ₹151.00 crore by 2024/2026.
  • Mismatched Sub-Totals & Blank Lines: Row 6 sums items 1 + 2.2 + 3.2 + 4.6 to get 10,039.67 lakhs (₹100.39 Cr), falsely citing Item 3.2 (₹100 lakh) while secretly adding Item 3.3 (₹1,411.55 lakh). Rows 7 (GST @ 18%), 10 (Ropeway Cost), 11 (Consultancy fee @ 8.0%), and 13 (Total Ropeway Cost) are left completely blank.
  • Phantom Consultancy Math: Row 12 inserts GST on consultancy @ 18% of (11) as ₹352.34 lakhs—which mathematically requires the blank consultancy fee in Row 11 to be ₹1,957.44 lakhs (₹19.57 crore), an impossible 19.5% of the entire project cost rather than the stated 8.0%.
  • Unlisted Contingency Base & Exclusions: Row 15 computes Contingency @ 3% as ₹314.7603 lakhs (3% of 10,492.01 lakhs, a number absent from the sheet), while the bottom footnote states: "Exclusions: Land, EIA, forest clearance, Consultancy etc."

C. Hydrological Risks at Lum Shyllong & PM Gati Shakti Geospatial Mandate

Table 17 of the 2022 MIG report confirms 80% of consulted residents flagged threats to natural water springs and debris dumping, yet Table 14 (p. 27) plans for 211.5 KLD (211,500 liters/day) of water extraction at the Lum Shyllong recharge zone (threatening Lawsohtun and Umjasai springs) based on an unsupported peak figure of 20,000 daily riders—versus 1,528–3,276 projected daily riders in Table 3.

Under Meghalaya Gazette Notification No. PLR.33/2022/Pt/485 (published September 11, 2025) and PM-DevINE Para 6.1, all projects exceeding ₹10 crore require mandatory GIS layer integration (.KML point, polyline, and polygon geometries) and Annexure-B multi-modal vetting on the PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan prior to administrative approval or expenditure sanction.
