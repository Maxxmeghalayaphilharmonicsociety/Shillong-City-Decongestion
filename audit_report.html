Forensic Audit & Fact-Check Report: Shillong Peak Ropeway Project

Independent cross-verification of internal project files, statutory notifications, and official November 2026 public consultation statements against publicly accessible records from the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEF&CC)[cite: 1], the New Development Bank (NDB)[cite: 1], the Ministry of DoNER[cite: 1], and the Government of Meghalaya[cite: 1].

Executive Audit Conclusion

A forensic audit comparing internal project documents against official public-domain filings confirms major institutional contradictions[cite: 1]. Public records verify that the 955-meter ropeway underwent federal Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) appraisal under Schedule Activity 7(g) in 2021[cite: 1], was formally pitched to both domestic urban planners and multilateral lenders as a road-traffic and greenhouse-gas emissions reduction measure[cite: 1], and faces direct statutory, co-financing, hydrological, and building-code conflicts[cite: 1].

01

Environmental Clearance (EIA) & Regulatory Appraisal

Official Claim (Nov 2026)

During the November 2026 public consultations, state representatives asserted that an Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) was never applicable or required for the ropeway project[cite: 1].

Internal Document Finding

Page 32 of the November 2022 MIG Public Consultation Report explicitly lists "EIA Clearances" as the very first mandatory statutory approval required before construction can begin[cite: 1].

Public-Domain Fact-Check

Official MoEF&CC records on the PARIVESH portal confirm the 955-meter Proposed Shillong Peak Ropeway was appraised at 66th & 73rd EAC Meetings under Schedule Item 7(g) of EIA Notification, 2006[cite: 1].

Critical Legal & Technical Nuance: While initial Environmental Clearance and Stage-I/II Forest Clearance were processed for the baseline 2021 scope (₹83.56 crore), major changes in project cost (escalating to ₹151 crore), massive multi-story commercial construction at both terminals (G+4 and G+3), and unresolved community spring objections trigger mandatory scrutiny[cite: 1].
02

The "Decongestion" & Urban Mobility Contradiction

Official Claim vs. Internal DPR: Officials stated at the November 2026 consultation that traffic decongestion was never part of the project rationale or policy documentation[cite: 1]. Yet Page 30 of the 2022 MIG Report explicitly states the project will "reduce the number of vehicle movement from Shillong town to Shillong Peak... reducing significantly the source of emission."[cite: 1]

Shillong Urban Mobility Policy (2024)

Section 6.2 explicitly integrates ropeways and cable cars as alternative public transit interventions to relieve congestion across Shillong's steep terrain[cite: 1].

NDB Sovereign Loan Filing (July 18, 2022)

The New Development Bank disclosure officially committed that "The Project will also contribute to reduced greenhouse gas emissions through replacement of road travels by the ropeway."[cite: 1]

Infrastructure Reality on the Ground: Despite this public transit framing, Table 12 of the 2022 MIG Report allocates 258 to 305 four-wheeler parking spaces at the Lower Terminal Point in Lawsohtun, concentrating tourist vehicular traffic inside residential neighborhoods[cite: 1].

03

Co-Financing & Non-Duplication Rules (NDB vs. PM-DevINE)

Public reporting confirms the ₹151 crore project is co-financed through ₹80.89 crore from the New Development Bank (NDB) and ₹70.11 crore from the Ministry of DoNER's PM-DevINE scheme[cite: 1]. Cross-checking this against PM-DevINE guidelines exposes a direct compliance conflict[cite: 1]:

1. New Development Bank Disclosure

Approved July 18, 2022 (USD 79.05M total loan package), the NDB project summary explicitly lists the Shillong Peak ropeway under Component (iii)[cite: 1].

2. PM-DevINE Non-Duplication Mandate

Under Section 2.2 and Section 7.1.5(ii) (Negative List), central grants are barred for projects funded by other line ministries without formal EIMC bifurcation approval[cite: 1].

04

Statutory Compliance Under the Meghalaya Ropeway Act, 2022

The Meghalaya Ropeway Act, 2022 (Act No. 14 of 2022), notified on September 27, 2022, imposes binding pre-construction safeguards across 65 sections[cite: 1]:

Provision Legal Requirement in Public Act Audit Finding / Compliance Gap
Section 3(2) & 2(k) Requires a Vision Paper at planning stage in consultation with Local Authority (Syiem, Lyngdoh, Rangbah Shnong)[cite: 1]. Consultation occurred reactively during 2026 disputes rather than via a statutory Vision Paper[cite: 1].
Sections 26, 29 & 30 Requires publishing draft licence, allowing 30 days for objections, and serving notice on landowners[cite: 1]. Publicly verifiable 30-day draft notices and individual ROW notices remain unproduced[cite: 1].
Sections 34 & 35 Mandates gazetted constitution of a District Project Committee chaired by DC[cite: 1]. Traditional bodies and the Joint Action Committee have been excluded from formal governance[cite: 1].
Section 36(2)–(4) Executing works over property requires written order and prior payment of compensation/annual rent[cite: 1]. The 19,100 sq. m. aerial corridor traverses community forests without Section 36 compensation orders[cite: 1].
05

Building Bye-Laws, Cost Anomalies & Geospatial Vetting

A. Building Bye-Law Violations (Gazette Sept 11, 2025)

Under the Meghalaya Building (Second Amendment) Bye-Laws, 2025, expedited self-certification applies strictly to low/medium-risk buildings, and Clause J.2(f) explicitly excludes State Government projects[cite: 1]. Yet the Lower Terminal proposes a G+4 commercial complex (20m height, 10,000 sq. m.) and the Upper Terminal proposes a G+3 structure (4,800 sq. m.) inside a protected forest zone, requiring full statutory scrutiny[cite: 1].

B. Cost Escalation (+80.7%) & Broken Arithmetic (MIG Report pp. 33–34)

  • Escalation Timeline: Appraised at ₹83.56 Cr in 2021, ₹108.06 Cr in Nov 2022, and ₹151.00 Cr by 2026[cite: 1].
  • Blank & Contradictory Rows: GST, ropeway cost, and consultancy rows are left blank while implying a phantom 19.5% consultancy fee (₹19.57 Cr)[cite: 1].

C. Hydrological Risks & PM Gati Shakti GIS Mandate

Table 17 confirms 80% of consulted residents flagged threats to natural springs, yet Table 14 plans for 211.5 KLD (211,500 L/day) water extraction at Lum Shyllong based on an unsupported 20,000 daily passenger figure[cite: 1]. Furthermore, projects >₹10 Cr require mandatory GIS layer integration on the PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan[cite: 1].
